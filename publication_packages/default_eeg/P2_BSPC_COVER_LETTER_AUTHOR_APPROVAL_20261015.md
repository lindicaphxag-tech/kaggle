Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our original research manuscript, **"Released EEG identifiers can share numerical recording contents: A full-dataset audit and matched evaluation of cross-subject decoding"**, for *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control*.

The work addresses a practical data-integrity assumption in EEG benchmarking: distinct released identifiers need not represent disjoint digital signal contents. We systematically audited a 50-identifier public motor-imagery EEG release, including all 1,225 cross-identifier pairs of EDF digital signals. Two released identifiers shared 40 identically processed labelled epochs, while other pairs exhibited partial or zero aligned digital overlap. Source MAT products did not justify inferring the same clinical participant or original acquisition. We separated these levels of provenance explicitly.

A matched 49-content-group retrospective evaluation distinguishes changes due to grouping reported scores from changes due to content-disjoint source refitting and parameter selection. Effects were localized, and we do not interpret the aggregate differences as an identified causal leakage penalty. Additional public EEG collections were used as fixed screening controls. We believe this transparent source-integrity audit and its implications for reproducible EEG decoding align with the journal's mission in biomedical signal analysis and benchmarking.

# SemRepair | Peer-reviewed precedent versus candidate flagship (10 Oct 2026)

**Question:** What does authority-aware repair provide beyond identification, probing, and already-published real-robot policy adaptation? Existing evidence: four *known* native MuJoCo worlds x 3 held excitation commands; 12 dependent conditions, full 12/12 correct authorization, external only 3/12; expected probe cost 4, exactly identical to independent optimal finite-class active identification. A separate counterfactual experiment constructs 24 commands x 3 fault worlds and wrong-interface compensating repairs, demonstrating that identical exterior trajectories do not imply the same permitted source patch. Neither study claims unseen robot/robot policy success.

| Existing published precedent | What peer-reviewed prior work actually establishes | Overlap/reviewer objection | Required distinguishing outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Robot Trains Robot: Automatic Real-World Policy Adaptation and Learning for Humanoids, CoRL 2025 (official CoRL program; https://2025.corl.org/program/main-conference ) | Real-world policy adaptation on robots, not just diagnostic toy model. | Repair study on 4 toy parameter worlds does not establish operational robotic applicability. | New controller/actuator family and *task-level success* with frozen repair rule; include explicit effect of incorrect interface patch. |
| Efficient Evaluation of Multi-Task Robot Policies with Active Experiment Selection, CoRL 2025 (https://2025.corl.org/program/main-conference ) | Quantifies the role of deliberate active experimentation and observation cost in robot policy evaluation. | Probe selection itself is not a new concept; the measured staged policy exactly matches the finite optimal active diagnosis oracle cost 4. | Prove and measure **repair-class authorization** under costs/risks where generic full identity learning cannot match, on unseen source family; matched observation permissions. |
| Geometric Red-Teaming for Robotic Manipulation, CoRL 2025 (https://mlanthology.org/corl/2025/ ) | Strong robotics papers analyze failures and targeted counterexamples rather than only nominal success. | One hand-authored simulator fault family is not enough for a general robustness claim. | Blind separate source-constructor, source-provenance attack, detection/refusal rates, report unsafe and unnecessary refusals. |

**Novelty positioning (narrow):** actionable *source-bound patch permission* for observationally equivalent robot interfaces, not generic active diagnosis or generic policy adaptation. The byte-digest guard is an engineering contract; it is not a secure hardware provenance primitive, race-free installation protocol, or proof of physical safety.

**Main results currently claimable:** controlled native physics counterexamples, bounded-identifiability and repair-authority preservation, matched optimal expected probe-cost parity. **Do NOT claim:** new state-of-the-art robot success, generic unknown-world transfer, improved active identification, source authentication in an adversarial execution system, or third-party adoption.

**Manuscript alignment with competitive CoRL/T-RO papers:** Fig 1 distinguish observation-equivalent world and different legal repair; Fig 2 exact finite minimax authorization construction; Fig 3 two separate controller families; Tab 1 independent-world and physical task outcomes; Tab 2 compare strong identifiability/decision-region baselines at equal readouts, probe costs and risk; Fig 4 real failure cases/no-authority branch. Novelty gate = cross-family safe-repair accuracy at comparable task success and defensible probe cost with no test-source leakage. Do not include a result figure before real outcomes exist.

**Editorial target:** IEEE T-RO/RA-L conditional on genuine robotic transfer; a scoped controlled-method/repair-protocol paper is more realistic without it. Publications above are **comparators and method examples**, not proof of a publication threshold or endorsement.

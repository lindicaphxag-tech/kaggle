\section{Source-Conditioned Authority Identification}\label{sec:authority}

\paragraph{A repair label is not a physical state label.}
Let $\mathcal H$ be a finite set of source-implementation/physical-world
hypotheses, $O:\mathcal H\to\mathcal O$ the entire available sensor
transcript (possibly including adaptively selected probes), and
$A:\mathcal H\to\mathcal R$ the \emph{permitted concrete repair interface}.
Two worlds may have the same physical response yet require different
software ownership or dependency repairs. A policy
$\pi:\mathcal O\to\mathcal R\cup\{\bot\}$ either proposes a repair or refuses,
where $\bot$ explicitly denotes abstention.

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (observation-only authority impossibility).}
There exists an observation-only, always-authorizing rule that is correct
on every $h\in\mathcal H$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decision-fiber}
\forall h,h'\in\mathcal H,\quad
O(h)=O(h')\implies A(h)=A(h').
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} If (\ref{eq:decision-fiber}) fails, a single observation
must be assigned two different repairs, a contradiction. Conversely,
if it holds, assign each nonempty observation fiber its unique repair.
$\square$
Consequently, a collision with two equally probable, different-authority
worlds incurs at least 50\% wrong repairs under any \emph{non-abstaining}
observation-only rule restricted to that collision. This is a finite-model
decision ambiguity statement, not an empirical population-risk bound.

\paragraph{Source-bound repair fields.}
Assume external trustworthy fields $g_j(h)$ are available at declared
costs $c_j\ge 0$ after source verification. For all sensor-aliased
world pairs requiring different repairs, define the conflict graph
\[
E=\{(h,h'):O(h)=O(h'),\ A(h)\ne A(h')\}.
\]
A minimal static authority-separating evidence set solves
\begin{align}
\min_{z\in\{0,1\}^{m}}&\quad \sum_{j=1}^m c_jz_j,\\
\textrm{s.t.}&\quad
\sum_{j:g_j(h)\ne g_j(h')}z_j\ge 1
\qquad \forall(h,h')\in E. \label{eq:provenance-cover}
\end{align}
Only \emph{verified} source fields may appear in the sum. The optimum
can differ from that of the stronger \emph{full system identity}
problem, obtained by requiring separation of all unequal world pairs
rather than only conflicts in $E$. This is a weighted set-cover
instance; active decision-region determination and diagnosis cost
optimization were already studied by Javdani et al.\ (AISTATS 2014)
and Cicalese et al.\ (ICML 2014). Our hypothesis is that
\emph{source verification and physical repair-effect testing} together
make this distinction operational in open-world robot control.

\paragraph{What the completed native MuJoCo experiment establishes.}
The frozen three-waveform, four-known-plus-four-unknown model has 24
dependent world-waveform conditions. The sensor-only closed-catalog
policy authorizes 15, 3 of which use the wrong source-bound repair,
and refuses 9. Requiring a \emph{hypothetically trustworthy} source
manifest authorizes 12 correctly and refuses 12, avoiding all three
observed wrong-authority decisions \emph{at a lower authorized
coverage}. A separate three-hypothesis illustrative source-version
case has optimal static authority-separating evidence cost 1 and
full-version identity cost 2 in \emph{hand-specified relative units}.
The continuous physical trajectories were genuinely stepped by
MuJoCo; the source IDs, repair rules, probe menus and costs were
constructed by the authors.

\paragraph{Nonnegotiable limitations and falsification.}
Equations~(\ref{eq:decision-fiber})--(\ref{eq:provenance-cover}) do
not solve unknown-source software attestation. If a foreign device
can impersonate a trusted issuer or the measurement agent is
compromised, the supposed verified fields provide no protection.
HMAC challenge-response tests are only controlled threat-model
checks with test fixture secrets, not secure boot or hardware
attestation. This finite test is not independent real-robot transfer,
not an improvement over the exact optimal classical active-diagnosis
expected probe cost (both cost 4 under the original frozen panel),
and not proof of task-level success or statistical safety.
The decisive experiment is a second independently implemented
controller family with externally frozen repair effects, probe
authorization contracts, source attestations, known/unknown
case splits and equal-budget decision-region baselines.

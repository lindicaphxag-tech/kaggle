\subsection{Separately released source MAT arrays: first-pass comparison}
\label{sec:p2-source-MAT}
To test whether the EDF-layer relationship
could be an artifact introduced solely
\emph{after} source-file publication, we
examined the independently distributed
Figshare v5 \texttt{sourcedata.zip} archive.
Its ZIP central directory identified
one MAT member for each of the six
previously flagged public identifiers.
Strict HTTP partial-content range requests
retrieved only those six selected members,
totalling about 228 MB of transfer
without downloading the full
1.875 GB archive. Their ZIP CRC values
were validated, and the full MAT
numerical contents were examined
within ephemeral runner storage
without publishing patient metadata
or waveform arrays.

Each of the six released MAT files
contained a large numeric array with
identical $40\times33\times4000$ shape.

correlations are:

The existence of high similarity in
the separately released source MAT
\emph{products} means the relationship
cannot safely be attributed exclusively
to our additional MOABB filtering
or to one EDF reader. However,
whole-array correlations can be distorted
by a single high-variance channel,
shared reference or data-export structure.
Thus these initial values do \emph{not}
prove that each EEG channel or each
motor-imagery trial is a near duplicate.
A subsequent channel-by-trial audit,
and the publisher's MAT--EDF channel
mapping, are needed before identifying
the signal-level mechanism.
The successful real-public-source
receipt is archived at
\url{https://github.com/lindicaphxag-tech/kaggle/actions/runs/38333234894}.
The authors have no access to clinical
acquisition lineage records; MAT filename
distinctness and array correlation do
not determine participant identity.

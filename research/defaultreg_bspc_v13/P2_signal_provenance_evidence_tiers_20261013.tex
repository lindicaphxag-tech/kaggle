\subsection{Release-layer versus original-acquisition provenance}
\label{sec:p2-public-provenance-tier}
The public Liu2024 description distinguishes original
acquisition data and processed signal products
\cite{liu2024stroke}. In the MOABB loader used here,
\texttt{data\_path} resolves to a
publisher-supplied \texttt{edffile} path; the
public dataset metadata classifies these EDF
signals as \emph{preprocessed} (including
baseline removal and 0.5--40~Hz filtering).
Our subsequent 8--30~Hz and resampling
pipeline is a \emph{further} transformation.
These layers must not be conflated.

The strictest current evidence is therefore
\emph{release-content} evidence at two levels:
(i) the additional MOABB-transformed labelled
epochs, and (ii) the stored EDF 16-bit
digital channel payload \emph{before}
our additional filtering. Using two libraries
or an independent digital integer reader on
the \emph{same EDF file} reduces software
reader error but does not introduce an
independent original acquisition.

The data release separately describes a
source-data MAT archive. Comparison of
the \emph{original acquisition MAT records}
against each other and against the
publisher's EDF export is a separate
uncompleted provenance gate. Even
agreement at that earlier data layer
would establish numerical signal ancestry,
not that two public IDs identify the same
clinical person or session. The latter
requires contextual, nonidentifying
confirmation from the dataset custodians.
No original MAT results or custodian replies
are reported in this study.

Consequently, our labels \emph{exact EDF
content overlap}, \emph{highly similar
EDF content} and \emph{unknown clinical
recording origin} designate different
scientific claims. In particular, a
conservative 47-group grouping exercise
is a source-exclusion sensitivity
scenario, not a count of 47 verified
independent physiological recordings.

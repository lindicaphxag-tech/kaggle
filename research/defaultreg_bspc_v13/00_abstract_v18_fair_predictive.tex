\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Objective:}
Source-participant discrimination and covariance-estimation
criteria can favour different regularisation strengths in
zero-target EEG decoding. We examine whether an apparent
transfer advantage reflects a genuine predictive-covariance
comparison or the precise validation estimand being scored.
\textbf{Methods:}
Using prospectively frozen participant-held EEG decoding
experiments, we audit the executed source-only covariance
scoring algorithm and compare its validation-class-centred
Gaussian log score with a proper class-conditional predictive
log score fitted using source-training class means.
We derive an exact nonnegative class-mean-shift penalty
that separates their objectives. A new Shin2017A source-only
predictive comparator was executed \emph{retrospectively}
against an immutable original 29-participant score grid.
\textbf{Results:}
The original EEGMMIDB prospective block ($N=20$)
showed a $+0.0561$ macro-AUC contrast between
source-discrimination and validation-class-centred
covariance selection (Holm $p=0.00597$), alongside
five participants harmed by more than 0.02 AUC.
Two frozen external BNCI comparisons were each
non-significant after their declared adjustments,
and a separate prospective spectral comparison
refuted a general filter-bank improvement.
For Shin2017A ($N=29$), the new proper-predictive
selection obtained 0.786207 mean AUC compared with
0.785900 for source-discrimination selection and
0.763678 for the original class-centred selector.
The new criterion selected $\lambda=0.2$ in
all 29 overlapping outer folds.
\textbf{Conclusion:}
The original covariance baseline was class-centred
and must not be called a proper held-source predictive
likelihood. Correcting its measurement target
removes the observed source-discrimination advantage
in one retrospective external analysis. The results
support transparent objective specification and
prospective replication, not a universal shrinkage
choice or participant-safe adaptation.
\end{abstract}

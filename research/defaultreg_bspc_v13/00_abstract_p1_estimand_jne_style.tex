\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Objective.}
In cross-person electroencephalography (EEG) decoding,
a validation-class-centred covariance score can be
mistaken for source-trained Gaussian predictive
likelihood. We distinguish these estimands and test
whether apparent superiority in source-only shrinkage
selection survives correction of this comparator.

\textbf{Approach.}
We audit participant-grouped source-only tangent-space
linear discriminant analysis using originally frozen
EEG transfer comparisons, a fixed 27-point
regularisation grid and explicit zero-target-information
budgets. An exact finite-sample identity decomposes
the difference between validation-class-centred and
proper predictive Gaussian quadratic scores. New
proper-predictive comparisons are designated
\emph{retrospective}, not incorporated into earlier
prospective inference families.

\textbf{Main results.}
In the prospectively frozen EEGMMIDB 20-person block,
source-macro-AUC selection exceeded the historical
class-centred CovScore selector by $0.0561$ AUC
(Holm $p=0.00597$), but harmed five participants by
more than $0.02$ AUC. Against the corrected
source-trained predictive criterion, the
\emph{post-outcome} paired AUC differences were
$-0.000307$ (Shin2017A, $n=29$) and
$+0.006819$ (BNCI2014\_002, $n=14$;
exploratory interval includes zero).
A separate retrospective BNCI2015\_001 12-person
comparison yielded $+0.035989$ AUC against the
corrected predictive comparator (paired exact
$p=0.1572$) but harmed three participants. On three
previously exposed source-validation acquisitions,
$98.59\%$, $99.29\%$ and $99.77\%$ of the
\emph{additional Gaussian mean-shift penalty} lay
in components invariant to the fixed classifier's
within-person AUC. The prespecified filter-bank
interaction contradicted the original direction
hypothesis ($-0.07781$ AUC).

\textbf{Significance.}
Covariance-fit, predictive-density and subject-level
discrimination claims cannot be interchanged.
Our findings yield a reproducible score-auditing
protocol; they do not establish a consistently
superior transfer decoder or an independently
validated policy for unknown EEG participants.
\end{abstract}

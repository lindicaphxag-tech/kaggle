\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Objective.}
Covariance fit and discrimination on previously unseen EEG participants
need not favour the same regularisation strength.
We ask which quantity a historically labelled
covariance negative-log-likelihood selector actually estimates and
which parts of the corrected predictive score can be relevant
to a fixed linear classifier's per-person ROC-AUC.

\textbf{Approach.}
We retain participant-grouped source-only, zero-target-information
selection and the original frozen EEG transfer evidence.
A class-centred held-source covariance score (CovScore) is
distinguished from proper source-trained-class-mean Gaussian
predictive likelihood. Their exact difference is partitioned
into common-location, score-orthogonal contrast and score-aligned
contrast shifts, with the classifier and feature reference fixed.
Previously exposed Cho2017, Dreyer2023A and BNCI2014\_002 acquisitions
are analysed as source-only explanatory cohorts, not new target
confirmations.

\textbf{Main results.}
In an originally prospectively frozen EEGMMIDB block of 20 participants,
the source-discrimination choice exceeded the historical
class-centred CovScore choice by $0.0561$ participant-macro
ROC-AUC (within-family Holm $p=0.00597$), but five participants
were materially harmed. In three separate \emph{retrospective}
source-validation acquisitions, $98.59\%$, $99.29\%$ and
$99.77\%$ of the \emph{additional mean-shift penalty}
measured by proper versus class-centred Gaussian scoring
was allocated to components invariant to the fixed
linear classifier's within-participant AUC.
Prospective- or protocol-first continuous/source-envelope
extensions did not establish repeatable improvements.

\textbf{Significance.}
EEG regularisation studies should distinguish validation
estimands and pre-target information budgets before claiming
a transferable decision improvement.
The decomposed score penalty is \emph{not} a percentage of
useful EEG information or classification gain.
The contribution is an independently auditable
EEG measurement finding, not a new SOTA decoder.
\end{abstract}

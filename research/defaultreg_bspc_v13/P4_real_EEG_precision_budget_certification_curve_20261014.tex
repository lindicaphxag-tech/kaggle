\subsection{Observed certificate precision budget on real final EEG features}
\label{sec:p4-real-feature-precision-curve}
The preceding real-final-feature test fixes the grid at
$2^{-18}$. To quantify whether certification is trivial
across \emph{all} floating-to-fixed point precisions,
we ran a source-code-identical, previously exposed
BNCI2014\_001 subject 1 sensitivity over
fractional-bit counts
$b\in\{2,4,6,8,10,14,18\}$.
The stored \emph{finite binary64 output} of the
two-electrode MNE/tangent feature pipeline
(64 balanced source epochs, three feature
coordinates) is represented as an exact rational
and independently rounded to the step $2^{-b}$.
An entrywise worst-case radius
$\eta_b=2^{-(b+1)}$ is checked by
an \emph{exact rational difference} for every
one of the 64$\times$3 features.
The unchanged 12 positive--negative sample pairs
were fixed in the earlier experiment, not
chosen to maximise this curve.

The same pooled-covariance and class-contrast
bounds of Section~\ref{sec:p4-final-feature-chain}
and the same eigenspace-invariant
resolvent certificate of
Section~\ref{sec:p4-cov-resolvent}
were applied at each precision.

All 84 resulting evaluations and their exact
input-error bounds were independently replayed
in successful
\href{https://github.com/lindicaphxag-tech/kaggle/actions/runs/38537814266}
{GitHub Actions run 38537814266}.
The threshold is \emph{empirical and input-specific}:
eight fractional bits suffice for all 12
\emph{these} comparisons; the theorem does not
identify eight bits as a general numerical
precision requirement. Because re-quantisation
changes the centre matrix as well as $\eta_b$,
the algorithm's count is not claimed to
be monotone for arbitrary inputs or
all possible precision budgets.
The original real EEG acquisition and all
prior preprocessing/eigendecomposition error
remain entirely \emph{outside} the proved
final-feature box. Thus this experiment
contributes a falsifiable stability-versus-
representational-resolution application,
not a stronger physical or patient-level
EEG correctness theorem, performance
improvement or independent source validation.

\subsection{A second new source-locked motor-imagery cohort: Dreyer2023B}
\label{sec:p3-new-dreyerb}
To examine task-specific variability without recycling
the previously exposed BNCI2014\_004 and Weibo2014
targets, we ran the same five policies on a newly
tracked \emph{Dreyer2023B} subset. This dataset is
related to the broader Dreyer2023 study; a distinct
Dreyer2023A subset had been examined in our
earlier research. Thus this constitutes a
new \emph{within-repository} held cohort and
not independent external preregistration or
a separately recruited research laboratory.

We fitted all source-only algorithms on IDs
61--68, using C3/Cz/C4, left/right motor imagery,
8--30 Hz, 160 Hz and exactly the same
source-fitted tangent geometry and model family.
The source choices were frozen to SHA-256
\texttt{3b2022007a22076951ae09f3ba4ce7e312edc37816ae62e13233179c90fca3aa}
\emph{before requesting either target}. With
no subsequent target hyperparameter selection,
we evaluated held IDs 69 and 70 (240 labelled
trials each). The completed code and untouched
within-run target receipts are archived in
\url{https://github.com/lindicaphxag-tech/kaggle/actions/runs/38390737635}.

SCE and anisotropic selection were worse than
the source macro on \emph{both} newly read people,
with an anisotropic loss exceeding $0.02$
AUC on held ID 69. Group-centred selection
gained less than $0.001$ AUC on each, while
continuous agreed exactly with the default.
This contrasts with the mild SCE improvement
observed on one newly read BNCI2015\_004
\emph{mental-task} participant. The
differences are descriptive across a few
different acquisition/task conditions:
they do not prove which dataset property
causes a sign reversal. We do \emph{not}
pool trials, target people or source folds
as independent acquisition-level calibration
units, and the available study does not
provide a valid conformal meta-acquisition
risk guarantee or a new clinical policy.
